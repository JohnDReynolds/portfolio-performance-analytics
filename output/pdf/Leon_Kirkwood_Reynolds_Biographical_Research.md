# Leon Kirkwood Reynolds (1889-1963)

## A documented biographical and genealogical research report

Prepared July 26, 2026

This report consolidates the information located to date about Leon Kirkwood
Reynolds, who was usually known as Kirk Reynolds and was sometimes recorded as
L. Kirk Reynolds or Leon K. Reynolds. It preserves both established facts and
promising unresolved clues. Evidence is distinguished from inference so that
later discoveries can be incorporated without losing the provenance of the
current conclusions.

The research subject is the grandfather of the person who requested this
report. Family testimony identifies Leon's wife as Philomene Barnes Reynolds
and Daniel Kirkwood Reynolds as their only child. It also preserves important
details about Leon's noncombat World War I service in France, the family's
Honolulu residence and travel, their separation immediately before Pearl
Harbor, and Leon's final illness. [S33]

---

## 1. Principal findings

The available documentary evidence and explicitly identified family testimony
support the following biographical profile:

- **Full name:** Leon Kirkwood Reynolds.
- **Names used publicly:** Kirk Reynolds, L. Kirk Reynolds, Leon K. Reynolds,
  and, in a cemetery transcription, Kirkwood Reynolds.
- **Birth:** December 9, 1889, in Arcadia, Valley County, Nebraska.
- **Move to Portland:** Approximately 1902, when Leon was about twelve or
  thirteen. His 1963 death notice said that he had moved to the Portland area
  61 years earlier.
- **World War I:** Family testimony states that Leon served in France during
  World War I but did not see combat. His branch, unit, dates, and duties have
  not yet been documented. [S33]
- **Parents:** George W. Reynolds was identified as his father in Leon's
  wedding announcement. A 1920 directory lists George W. with a wife named
  Isabelle, making Isabelle Leon's probable mother. The biological relationship
  should still be verified with a census, birth record, or death certificate.
- **Sibling:** Irene H. Reynolds, a music teacher, was explicitly identified as
  Leon's sister and served as maid of honor at his wedding.
- **Marriage:** Leon married Philomene Barnes at 5:00 p.m. on March 3, 1921, at
  the Irvington home of her father, E. L. Barnes.
- **Only child:** Daniel Kirkwood Reynolds, born April 7, 1932, in Portland,
  was Leon and Philomene's only child according to family testimony. [S25, S33]
- **Philomene's earlier life:** Family testimony places Philomene first in
  Michigan, then in Spokane, and later in Portland. Her father, E. L. Barnes,
  had a sawmill in Spokane that burned down. [S33]
- **Occupation and career:** Mortgage-loan inspection, savings-and-loan
  association work, statewide industry leadership, Federal Housing
  Administration underwriting, Federal Home Loan Bank governance, and
  presidency of First Federal Savings and Loan Association of Portland.
- **Honolulu:** The family lived in a government bungalow very near the Royal
  Hawaiian Hotel and traveled between San Francisco and Honolulu by passenger
  ship. Philomene and Daniel returned to Portland shortly before December 7,
  1941, while Leon remained in Honolulu. A 1940 directory independently lists
  Kirk as the FHA chief underwriter, residing at the Colonial Hotel. [S16, S33]
- **Death:** June 11, 1963, at Providence Hospital in Portland, aged 73.
  Family testimony states that Leon had Parkinson's disease, suffered a
  fractured hip, and died from pneumonia in the hospital. [S01, S33]
- **Burial:** Clackamas Cemetery in Clackamas County, Oregon, in the area
  transcribed as "Left of Road."
- **Wife's burial:** Philomene B. Reynolds is recorded beside or in the same
  cemetery area, with the years 1889-1988.

An especially important correction is that **1963 is Leon's death year**.
The year 1988 belongs to Philomene. A secondary family-tree or index entry
appears to have incorrectly attached Philomene's death year to Leon.

## 2. Evidence and confidence conventions

The report uses the following practical confidence categories:

- **Established:** Supported by a contemporary record that identifies Leon
  directly, or by two or more mutually reinforcing records.
- **Strongly supported:** The evidence is highly persuasive but a record uses
  an abbreviated name or omits one identifying detail.
- **Family testimony:** Information supplied by a direct descendant. Family
  testimony is valuable evidence, particularly for movements and experiences
  not recorded in directories, but it is identified separately from
  documentary confirmation.
- **Provisional clue:** A potentially useful lead that should not yet be treated
  as part of the proven family tree.

Historic directories and newspapers were frequently searched through optical
character recognition. OCR errors are common, particularly with names in
small type. The absence of a search result is therefore not evidence that a
person was absent from a place.

## 3. Names and identity

The same individual appears under several forms:

- **Leon Kirkwood Reynolds** - the full name used in Daniel Kirkwood Reynolds's
  obituary and confirmed by the family.
- **Leon K. Reynolds** - the form used in the 1920 Portland directory.
- **Kirk Reynolds** - the overwhelmingly common professional and social form
  used in newspapers and later directories.
- **L. Kirk Reynolds** - the form used in the 1963 Oregonian death notice.
- **Kirkwood Reynolds** - the form in the Clackamas Cemetery transcription.

These variations explain why ordinary searches for "Leon Reynolds" initially
missed many of the most informative records.

No middle name other than Kirkwood has been found. The evidence does not
suggest that Leon and Kirk were different people. The continuity of spouse,
address, occupation, relatives, and dates establishes that these are name
variants for one man.

## 4. Birthplace and Nebraska background

Leon's death notice reports that he was born on **December 9, 1889, in
Arcadia, Nebraska**. Arcadia is a village in Valley County in central Nebraska.
The official Valley County website includes Arcadia among the county's
communities. [S01, S28]

The precise Reynolds household in Arcadia has not yet been proven. A
potentially significant but unresolved clue appears in the 1890 Nebraska State
Gazetteer. Its Arcadia entries include:

- An **Isabel Reynolds**, identified as Mrs. and serving as postmaster.
- A **W. B. Reynolds**, involved in real estate and insurance.

These entries are intriguing because Leon was born in Arcadia in 1889 and his
probable mother was later listed as Isabelle Reynolds. They are not sufficient
to identify either person as Leon's parent or relative. In particular, Leon's
1921 wedding announcement names George W. Reynolds as his father. Possible
explanations include unrelated Reynolds households, a remarriage or stepfamily,
an initial or transcription problem, or a more distant family relationship.
No one of these explanations should be adopted without census or vital-record
evidence. [S29]

The 1963 death notice says Leon moved to the Portland area approximately
61 years before his death. Subtracting 61 years from 1963 places the move
around **1902**. Leon would have been twelve, approaching thirteen. The wording
suggests that his family, not Leon alone, made the move. [S01]

No reliable record of Leon's elementary or secondary school has been located.
No college attendance has been established. His later technical expertise in
mortgage inspection and underwriting could have resulted from formal education,
professional training, work experience, or some combination, but the present
evidence does not distinguish among those possibilities.

Family testimony states that Leon served in France during World War I but did
not see combat. No draft card, service record, unit history, transport list,
or other military document has yet been located. His service should therefore
be preserved as family testimony while the branch, unit, rank, dates, duties,
and reason for being in France remain subjects for documentary research. A
World War I draft registration card would also be valuable because it should
provide Leon's exact address, occupation, employer, physical description, and
nearest relative between 1917 and 1918. [S33]

## 5. The Reynolds household in Portland

The 1920 Portland city directory provides the clearest early household record.
At **950 East Davis Street**, it lists:

- George W. Reynolds, with wife Isabelle, employed as a salesman.
- Irene H. Reynolds, a music teacher.
- Leon K. Reynolds.

All were recorded at the same address. [S03]

The 1921 wedding announcements strengthen the household interpretation.
They describe Leon as the son of Mr. and Mrs. George W. Reynolds of Portland.
They also identify Irene Reynolds as Leon's sister. [S05, S06]

The combined evidence establishes:

- George W. Reynolds was Leon's father, or at minimum the man publicly
  identified as his father at the time of Leon's marriage.
- Isabelle was George's wife in 1920 and was probably Leon's mother.
- Irene H. Reynolds was Leon's sister.

The present evidence does not establish George's or Isabelle's birthplaces,
ages, occupations before Portland, marriage date, or dates of death. Nor does
it show whether Leon had additional siblings.

## 6. Philomene Barnes and the Barnes family

Philomene Barnes was born in 1889. The cemetery transcription records the
years 1889-1988. A secondary genealogical index gives the more precise dates
September 5, 1889, to March 30, 1988, but those exact dates have not yet been
verified against an original death certificate, obituary, or Social Security
record. They should therefore be treated as provisional. [S02, S30]

Philomene's father was **E. L. Barnes**, whose Irvington home was used for the
1921 wedding. Her brother was **Maurice H. Barnes**. Maurice's wife was
**Geraldine Coursen Barnes**. [S05, S06]

Family testimony places Philomene first in **Michigan**, then in **Spokane,
Washington**, and later in **Portland, Oregon**. It also states that her
father, E. L. Barnes, had a sawmill in Spokane and that the sawmill burned
down. The precise Michigan location, whether Michigan was her birthplace, the
dates of the family moves, the sawmill's business name and location, and the
date and circumstances of the fire have not yet been documented. Newspaper,
city-directory, census, land, insurance, and business-license records may make
this family memory independently traceable. [S33]

A 1913 society report placed E. L. Barnes and Miss Philomene Barnes at Hotel
Bayocean. This helps establish Philomene's pre-marriage association with her
father and Portland society. [S08]

An earlier 1907 newspaper item lists a Mrs. L. Barnes, Maurice Barnes, and
Philomene Barnes among Portland visitors. The grouping is suggestive but does
not safely identify Mrs. L. Barnes as Philomene's mother. [S08]

The current evidence does not establish Philomene's mother's full name,
Philomene's precise birthplace, or her education. These details, and the
Michigan-Spokane-Portland sequence, may appear on her 1988 death certificate,
marriage license, obituary, or early census records.

## 7. Courtship, engagement, and wedding

Leon was socially associated with Philomene and her family by at least 1919.
A Sunday Oregonian society notice includes Philomene Barnes and E. L. Barnes
with a name OCR rendered as "Lem K. Reynolds." In context, this is probably a
misreading of Leon K. or Kirk Reynolds. Because the image has not been
independently transcribed at high resolution, the item is best treated as a
strong but not absolute identification. [S08]

The engagement of Philomene Barnes and Kirk Reynolds was formally announced in
February 1921. [S04]

They married at **5:00 p.m. on Thursday, March 3, 1921**, at the home of
Philomene's father, E. L. Barnes, in Portland's Irvington neighborhood.
The Rev. Edward H. Pence of Westminster Presbyterian Church officiated.
[S05, S06, S07]

The wedding party and music included:

- **Irene Reynolds**, Leon's sister, as maid of honor.
- **Maurice H. Barnes**, Philomene's brother, as best man.
- **Geraldine Coursen Barnes**, Maurice's wife, playing the wedding march.
- **Madeline Stone**, singing.

Leon and Philomene received guests with E. L. Barnes and Mr. and Mrs.
George W. Reynolds. After a wedding trip, the couple intended to make their
home in Portland. [S05, S06]

The Oregon marriage index records the couple as:

- Reynolds, Leon K.
- Barnes, Philomene M.
- Year 1921, volume 51, page 230.

The middle initial M. in the index should be preserved as recorded, although
later sources often use Philomene B., apparently treating Barnes as a middle
or maiden-name initial. [S07]

In October 1922, Mrs. Kirk Reynolds was among the women presiding at the tea
urns during a prominent Portland social event at the home of Mrs. Arch J.
Tourtellotte. The hostesses were Laura and Mabel Korell. The other women
presiding included Mrs. Neagle P. Sealy, Mrs. Harold Temple, and Mrs. Arthur
Fender. The notice places Philomene in the city's social network shortly after
her marriage. [S09]

## 8. Son: Daniel Kirkwood Reynolds

Daniel Kirkwood Reynolds was born in Portland on **April 7, 1932**, to
Philomene Barnes Reynolds and Leon Kirkwood Reynolds. He died January 28, 2025.
[S25]

Family testimony identifies Daniel as **Leon and Philomene's only child**.
No contradictory evidence has been found. [S33]

Daniel attended Lincoln High School and graduated from the University of Oregon
in 1955 with a Bachelor of Business Administration degree. He belonged to
Delta Upsilon fraternity, participated in ROTC, and later served in the United
States Army for eight years. [S25, S26]

After graduation, Daniel married **Norma Frances Crowley**. Their children,
as named in Daniel's published obituary, were:

- Catherine Reynolds Mock.
- John Daniel Reynolds.
- Susan Reynolds Workman.

Daniel became a residential real-estate appraiser. The 1959 Portland directory
specifically lists him as an appraiser for **Benjamin Franklin Federal Savings
and Loan Association**, with a residence in Vancouver, Washington. Later, he
formed his own appraisal company. In the 1980s he developed a commercial
computer service that assembled and distributed residential-sales data across
the Pacific Northwest. [S22, S25]

Daniel's career followed the same broad field as Leon's: housing finance,
property analysis, appraisal, and mortgage-lending information. This
intergenerational continuity is a documented family pattern rather than merely
a coincidence of employer names.

Daniel's obituary also records his interests in amateur photography, a home
darkroom, handcrafted slot-car tracks, humor, and sustained attention to the
daily lives and goals of his children and grandchildren. [S25]

## 9. Early career in mortgage lending

### 1925: Equitable Savings and Loan

The first direct professional records located are from 1925.

On March 25, 1925, a Klamath Falls newspaper noted Kirk Reynolds of Portland
in town on business. The brief item did not identify his employer. [S10]

On October 20, 1925, the Roseburg News-Review identified Kirk Reynolds as a
representative of **Equitable Savings and Loan**, visiting from Portland on
business. [S11]

Four days later, the Medford Mail Tribune described him more precisely as an
**inspector of loans for the Equitable Savings and Loan Association**, spending
several days in Medford on business. [S12]

These reports show that by age 35 Leon was traveling throughout Oregon to
inspect or supervise mortgage loans. Loan inspection would have required
judgment about property, collateral, valuation, construction, borrower risk,
and compliance with association policy. It established the professional base
for his later work in government underwriting.

Equitable Savings and Loan, First Federal Savings and Loan of Portland, and
Benjamin Franklin Savings and Loan were distinct institutions in contemporary
federal listings. Similar institutional names and later acquisitions make it
important not to merge their histories without evidence. [S23]

## 10. Depression-era savings-and-loan leadership

### 1932: Home Loan Bank advocacy

In October 1932, amid the Depression's mortgage and construction crisis, Kirk
Reynolds and Ben Hazen returned from a statewide meeting of savings-and-loan
officials. The Oregon Statesman reported their support for making the Home Loan
Bank in Portland operational. Their position was that access to the bank would
relieve pressure on local associations and allow building activity to resume.
[S13]

This activity places Leon in a broader financial-policy role. He was not only
evaluating individual loans; he was participating in efforts to stabilize the
institutions that supplied home mortgages.

### 1933: Executive secretary of the Oregon association

In February 1933, the Oregon Statesman identified Kirk Reynolds as
**executive secretary of the Oregon association of savings-and-loan
organizations**. He was in Salem monitoring Senate Bill 119. The legislation
would enable Oregon associations to become members of the Home Loan Bank and
obtain cash for new loans by discounting mortgages already in their portfolios.
[S14]

The practical issue was liquidity. An association might own sound mortgages
but lack cash to make new loans or meet pressure from savers. Home Loan Bank
membership offered a way to borrow against those mortgage assets. Leon's
involvement shows a working understanding of both institutional finance and
housing policy.

The reports do not establish whether his executive-secretary position was
full-time, part-time, or concurrent with another employer.

## 11. Federal Housing Administration work

### 1935: Acting chief underwriter in Portland

A March 1, 1935 Oregon Statesman article identifies Kirk Reynolds as
**acting chief underwriter for the Federal Housing Administration** in
Portland. [S15]

He was communicating with the Salem Realty Board about an FHA real-property
survey. Salem was to be the only Oregon city included in that 1935 survey.
The work involved an inventory of real property and a detailed study of the
local housing situation. Reynolds expected to travel from the Portland office
before March 10 and indicated that local real-estate professionals might be
asked to assist.

The position is significant for several reasons:

- It demonstrates federal housing responsibility soon after the FHA's creation
  under the National Housing Act of 1934.
- It shows advancement from private loan inspection to senior underwriting.
- It supplies a direct occupational bridge to the 1940 Honolulu directory.
- It places Leon at the intersection of appraisal, housing-market analysis,
  mortgage risk, and public policy.

The article uses the word "acting," so it should not be assumed that he already
held the permanent Portland chief-underwriter title.

## 12. Honolulu, 1939-1941

Family testimony gives a more precise account of the Honolulu period. Leon,
Philomene, and Daniel lived there for approximately two years before the
United States entered World War II. Their home was described within the family
as a **"government bungalow" very near the Royal Hawaiian Hotel**. The family
traveled between San Francisco and Honolulu aboard passenger ships. [S33]

Shortly before the December 7, 1941 attack on Pearl Harbor, **Philomene and
Daniel returned to Portland while Leon remained in Honolulu**. Leon was
therefore still in Honolulu at the time of the attack. His later travel date
and the circumstances of his return to Portland have not yet been established.
This more detailed testimony corrects the earlier, less precise impression
that the entire family returned together after the attack. [S33]

The strongest documentary corroboration is the 1940 Polk-Husted directory for
Honolulu and the Territory of Hawaii. Page 533 contains an entry in the
Reynolds alphabetical section for a **Kirk** who was **chief underwriter for
the FHA** and resided at the **Colonial Hotel**. [S16]

The searchable snippet reads in abbreviated directory form:

> Kirk, chief underwriter, FHA; residence Colonial Hotel.

This is a strongly supported identification of Leon because:

1. Kirk was the name Leon consistently used.
2. Leon was already documented as an FHA acting chief underwriter in Portland
   in 1935.
3. The family independently remembered his presence in Honolulu during the
   same period.
4. Mortgage underwriting and territorial FHA administration were natural
   extensions of his established career.

The possibility of an unrelated Kirk Reynolds simultaneously serving as an FHA
chief underwriter in Honolulu is remote. Nevertheless, the directory omits
"Leon" and "Kirkwood," so the report preserves the technical distinction
between a conclusive full-name record and an overwhelmingly persuasive match.

The Colonial Hotel listing and the family memory of a government bungalow
need not conflict. The directory could preserve temporary lodging, an initial
residence, a mailing address, an address supplied at a different point in the
assignment, or information collected before or after the bungalow residence.
It does not prove that Philomene and Daniel lived at the hotel. The phrase
"government bungalow" may describe federally supplied housing connected to
Leon's FHA post, but that interpretation remains to be documented. [S33]

Exact-name searches in the 1940 directory did not locate Philomene or Daniel.
This absence is not decisive:

- A spouse and minor child might not receive separate alphabetical entries.
- The family's household could have been recorded under an address section not
  visible through snippet search.
- Directory OCR and snippet indexing are incomplete.

The Library of Congress has a Honolulu telephone-directory compilation covering
November 1938 through approximately fall 1941 or 1942. A partial OCR search did
not locate Leon in the first directory segment; service rate limits prevented
a complete page-by-page search during this research session. [S17]

The 1941 Polk-Husted Honolulu directory exists, but its Google Books copy is not
searchable or previewable. Its Reynolds page remains an important target.

No passenger manifest, travel notice, employment order, government-housing
record, or Portland return notice has yet been found. Therefore:

- **Presence in Honolulu in 1940:** strongly documented.
- **FHA chief-underwriter role:** strongly documented.
- **Approximately two-year family stay:** family testimony, highly consistent
  with the directory evidence.
- **Government bungalow near the Royal Hawaiian Hotel:** family testimony,
  not yet tied to a street address or housing record.
- **Passenger-ship travel through San Francisco:** family testimony, not yet
  tied to particular vessels or sailing dates.
- **Philomene and Daniel returned before December 7, 1941:** family testimony,
  not yet tied to a passenger manifest.
- **Leon remained in Honolulu during the attack:** family testimony, strongly
  consistent with the timing and purpose of his documented assignment. [S33]

The Honolulu assignment may have been one of the most consequential periods of
Leon's career. As chief underwriter in a United States territory undergoing
rapid military and civilian development, he would likely have evaluated or
supervised mortgage-insurance risk under unusually active housing conditions.
That description is a reasoned occupational interpretation, not a claim about
specific projects.

## 13. Postwar and regional leadership

The present record has a gap between Leon's return from Honolulu and the early
1950s. Leon's exact return date, employer, and title during the 1942-1952
period have not yet been established.

### 1953: Federal Home Loan Bank director

The Housing and Home Finance Agency's annual report for calendar year 1953
lists **Kirk Reynolds** as a **Class C director of the Federal Home Loan Bank
of San Francisco**. [S18]

The Federal Home Loan Bank of San Francisco served a western regional network.
Board membership demonstrates that Leon's professional standing extended
beyond a single Portland association.

### 1955: Continuing bank participation

A March 1955 Eugene Register-Guard report again identifies Kirk Reynolds of
Portland as a director of the Federal Home Loan Bank of San Francisco. It
places him at the annual stockholders' meeting and records agreement among
participants that housing activity was rising. [S19]

Together, the 1953 and 1955 records show that the directorship was not merely a
one-day appointment or mistaken directory entry.

- Husband of Philomene B.
- Resident of **3635 SW Beaverton Avenue**.
- Telephone **CApitol 8-4524**. [S20]

The association's own directory listing also names Kirk Reynolds as president.

The 1957 Portland directory again lists Kirk and Philomene at 3635 SW
Beaverton Avenue. [S21]

The 1963 Oregonian death notice describes Leon as the retired **president and

- Whether he returned directly to First Federal after Honolulu.

- The date he retired.

## 16. The Benjamin Franklin Savings and Loan question

Family memory associates Leon with Benjamin Franklin Savings and Loan. The
documentary record found so far supports meaningful Benjamin Franklin
connections but does not yet show Leon as a direct employee or officer of that
institution.

### Facts directly documented

- In 1925 Leon represented and inspected loans for **Equitable Savings and
  Loan**. [S11, S12]
- In 1932 Leon participated in statewide savings-and-loan work with **Ben
  Hazen**. [S13]
- A congressional history identifies Hazen as the original
  secretary-treasurer and manager, later president and chairman, of the
  Portland association that became Benjamin Franklin Federal Savings and Loan.
  [S24]
- In the 1950s Leon was president of **First Federal Savings and Loan
  Association of Portland**. [S20]
- In 1959 Leon's son Daniel was an appraiser for **Benjamin Franklin Federal
  Savings and Loan Association**. [S22]
- Contemporary federal lists treat Equitable, First Federal, and Benjamin
  Franklin as separate Portland institutions. [S23]

### Best current interpretation

The family had an unquestionable Benjamin Franklin connection through Daniel,
and Leon worked directly with Benjamin Franklin's senior leadership through the
state savings-and-loan association and broader Home Loan Bank network. A direct
Leon-Benjamin Franklin employment relationship remains possible but has not
been located.

The family memory should not be discarded. It may preserve:

- A short or interim role omitted from the directories examined.
- Board, committee, consulting, or association work rather than ordinary
  employment.
- Leon's close collaboration with Ben Hazen.
- Daniel's long-term employment, later remembered as part of the family's
  shared savings-and-loan history.
- A later institutional relationship or succession not yet traced.

The next best sources are First Federal and Benjamin Franklin corporate
minutes, employee publications, annual reports, trade-journal notices, and
Leon's full obituary.

## 17. Death, funeral, and burial

The June 12, 1963 Oregonian death notice supplies the exact vital information.
It identifies **L. Kirk Reynolds, age 73**, of Willamette Manor, and describes
him as the retired president and manager of First Federal Savings and Loan
Company. It reports that he died Tuesday at Providence Hospital. Since the
notice appeared Wednesday, June 12, the death occurred on **June 11, 1963**.
[S01]

Family testimony supplies the medical context absent from the available death
notice preview. Leon had **Parkinson's disease**, suffered a **fractured hip**,
and was hospitalized in Portland. He died from **pneumonia** in the hospital.
The recollection supports a sequence of chronic Parkinson's disease, hip
fracture, hospitalization, and fatal pneumonia, but it does not establish the
clinical relationship among those conditions or their wording on the death
certificate. [S33]

The notice states:

- Birth on December 9, 1889, in Arcadia, Nebraska.
- Arrival in the Portland area approximately 61 years earlier.
- A funeral scheduled for 11:30 a.m. Thursday at Finley's Morninglight Chapel.
- A private committal, with the visible preview cutting off part of the final
  wording.

Thursday was June 13, 1963.

The Clackamas Cemetery transcription records:

- **Reynolds, Kirkwood**, born 1889, died 1963.
- **Reynolds, Philomene B.**, born 1889, died 1988.
- Location: **Section "Left of Road."** [S02]

The cemetery's use of "Kirkwood" rather than Leon or Kirk explains another
search difficulty. It also provides independent confirmation that Leon died in
1963 and that Philomene was the spouse who died in 1988.

The transcription provides years rather than exact dates and may reflect
either grave markers or a cemetery survey. A cemetery office record or marker
photograph could reveal the precise plot and marker wording.

## 19. Professional significance

Leon's career can be understood as a consistent progression through the
institutions that financed American home ownership:

1. **Loan inspection:** He evaluated individual mortgage loans for Equitable.
2. **Industry administration:** He helped represent Oregon savings-and-loan
   associations during the Depression.
3. **Liquidity and institutional policy:** He advocated Home Loan Bank
   membership and access to funding secured by existing mortgages.
4. **Federal underwriting:** He became a senior FHA underwriter in Portland and
   apparently chief underwriter in Honolulu.
5. **Regional governance:** He served on the Federal Home Loan Bank of San
   Francisco board.
6. **Executive leadership:** He became president and manager of First Federal
   Savings and Loan in Portland.

This was not a series of unrelated jobs. Each role involved the valuation,
funding, insurance, regulation, or institutional management of residential
mortgage credit.

Leon's Depression-era activity is particularly noteworthy. Savings-and-loan
associations could hold valuable mortgages but still lack cash for new lending
or withdrawals. The Home Loan Bank system provided a mechanism for turning
eligible mortgage assets into institutional liquidity. Leon was participating
in that transition while it was being implemented in Oregon.

His FHA role also placed him near the beginning of modern federally supported
mortgage underwriting. The FHA was created in 1934; Leon was already acting
chief underwriter in Portland in early 1935.

## 24. Overall assessment

Leon Kirkwood Reynolds was a Nebraska-born Portland housing-finance
professional whose career developed alongside the modern American
mortgage-finance system.

Beginning as a traveling loan inspector, he became a statewide
savings-and-loan representative during the Depression, an early senior
underwriter for the newly created Federal Housing Administration, an apparent
territorial FHA chief underwriter in Honolulu, a director of the Federal Home
Loan Bank of San Francisco, and president and manager of First Federal Savings
and Loan in Portland.

The family account of Honolulu is strongly corroborated by the 1940 directory.
The more detailed family testimony places the household in a government
bungalow near the Royal Hawaiian Hotel, records passenger-ship travel through
San Francisco, and establishes that Philomene and Daniel left before the Pearl
Harbor attack while Leon remained in Honolulu. These details create specific,
testable targets for passenger, housing, and federal-personnel records.

The family's recollection of Benjamin Franklin also has an identifiable
documentary foundation through Leon's collaboration with Ben Hazen and Daniel's
employment there, although Leon's precise direct relationship to that
institution remains unresolved.

The most important next document is Leon's 1963 death certificate. It has the
best chance of resolving his parents, the Arcadia household, his precise
residence, and the remaining vital-record questions in a single authoritative
source. It may also corroborate the family's recollection that pneumonia
followed a fractured hip in the setting of Parkinson's disease.

This report should be treated as a living research document. Its established
chronology is now strong, while its explicitly labeled clues provide a
disciplined starting point for the next stage of family research.
